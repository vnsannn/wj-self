Who is William James?

  • Born January 11, 1842 in New York City
  • Father of American Psychology
  • Leader of Pragmatism (philosophy) and founder of Functionalism (psychology)
  • Harvard Professor with a physician background before moving to psychology
  • Died August 26, 1910 in Chocorua, New Hampshire
02

The Principles of Psychology

  • Took 12 years to write, aimed to make psychology a natural science
  • Stream of Consciousness: personal, continuous, selective
  • Self as both Knower and Known: observer and the thing being observed at once
  • Functionalism applied to the self: what the self is for, not just what it is
First Edition, 1890
03

I-self (Self as Subject)

  • Also called the “pure ego” or the observer, the one who knows
  • Gives continuity, distinctness, and volition
  • Can never be caught as an object of study, the moment you try to observe it, it becomes Me-self instead
“The self of all the selves is the stream of conscious experience, the thought as it passes.” William James

05

Summary: Two Sides of the Same Self

  • I-self and Me-self are two sides of the same self, not two separate selves
  • I-self = the knower (subject); Me-self = the known (object)
  • Both work together to form one's complete sense of identity
